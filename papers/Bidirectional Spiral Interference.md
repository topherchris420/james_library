Bidirectional Logarithmic Spiral Interference as a
Geometric Substrate for Emergent Gravity
Christopher Woodyard
Vers3Dynamics
United States
christopher@vers3dynamics.com
Abstract—This paper proposes a geometric field model in
which gravitational-like organization may emerge from a bidi-
rectional logarithmic spiral interference lattice. Two oppositely
oriented logarithmic spirals sharing a common origin are treated
as phase-carrying trajectories within a dynamical field. Their
superposition produces a recursive network of intersections, con-
structive interference nodes, and curvilinear transport pathways.
These features are interpreted as coherent structural regions
within an underlying pre-geometric medium. While the model
remains speculative and does not derive known gravitational field
equations, it provides a structured framework for exploring how
spatial organization, stability, and effective attraction might arise
from interference geometry. Potential simulation approaches and
testable implications are discussed.
Index Terms—Emergent gravity, logarithmic spiral, interfer-
ence lattice, geometric physics, field coherence, self-similarity,
dynamical systems
I. INTRODUCTION
Gravity is conventionally described as the curvature of
spacetime in general relativity [1], while modern theory also
treats it as an effective field theory below a cutoff scale [8].
Several approaches suggest gravity may be emergent rather
than fundamental. Thermodynamic arguments relate gravita-
tional dynamics to entropy and local equilibrium [2]–[5]. Com-
plementary models propose entropic or induced mechanisms
[6], [7], and analogue-gravity systems demonstrate that metric-
like behavior can arise in non-gravitational media [9], [10].
More recent work connects spacetime structure to quantum
information and entanglement [11], [12]. These perspectives
motivate exploration of geometric or wave-based substrates
capable of producing large-scale organization.
This paper introduces a candidate substrate based on inter-
ference between two counter-oriented logarithmic spirals. The
goal is not to replace general relativity, but to explore whether
structured interference geometry can produce persistent orga-
nization and effective attraction.

III. PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION
Constructive interference regions correspond to coherent
nodes. Such interference-based structuring is consistent with
known wave behavior, where superposition of coherent waves
produces stable patterns of constructive and destructive in-
terference. Similar structures arise in wave interference and
singularity formation [15], [16], where standing-wave patterns
generate stable nodal configurations. These nodes may act as
persistent structural features within the field. Here, coherence
density is quantified locally as the squared magnitude of the
superposed field, i.e., |Ψ(r, θ)|2
, serving as a direct measure
of constructive interference strength.
Gradients in node density and field coherence define pre-
ferred pathways through the system. Test particles or effective
excitations propagating through the field are assumed to follow
effective trajectories determined by these gradients, producing
a transport bias toward regions of higher coherence.

IV. EMERGENT GRAVITY INTERPRETATION
Under this framework, gravitational-like behavior (specifi-
cally, effective attractive behavior) may arise from:
• gradients in coherence density,
• recursive reinforcement of interference nodes,
• path-dependent transport through the lattice.
This interpretation aligns conceptually with emergent gravity
approaches in which macroscopic dynamics arise from deeper
statistical or geometric structure [4]–[6].
V. SIMULATION APPROACH
A computational program includes:
1) generating spiral fields,
2) assigning phase evolution,
3) computing interference patterns,
4) identifying nodes and coherence gradients,
5) simulating effective particle trajectories.
Nodes are detected computationally as local maxima of
|Ψ(r, θ)|2
exceeding a chosen threshold, optionally combined
with phase-vortex criteria. Standard numerical methods such
as finite-difference, spectral, and FFT-based techniques may
be applied [17]–[20].
These simulations provide a means to evaluate whether
stable node clustering and trajectory curvature emerge robustly
across parameter regimes. A key question is whether stable
node clustering emerges dynamically rather than being im-
posed by initial conditions.
VI. LIMITATIONS
This model does not derive Einstein’s field equations, es-
tablish relativistic invariance, or define a complete physical
interpretation of Ψ. It remains a speculative geometric hypoth-
esis. In particular, the proposed dynamics are illustrative rather
than derived from a fundamental action principle. The model
does not currently specify boundary conditions or conservation
laws for Ψ, which would be required for a complete physical
theory.
VII. CONCLUSION
Bidirectional spiral interference provides a structured can-
didate framework for emergent gravitational organization. By
combining scale-invariant geometry with interference-based
coherence, the model offers a testable pathway for explor-
ing how effective attraction may arise from underlying field
structure.
Establishing a rigorous connection to known physical theo-
ries remains an open challenge and a key direction for future
work, particularly through numerical simulation, formal dy-
namical development, and comparison with analogue physical
systems.
